## 1. Colombian regulation

### 1.1 Habeas Data: Ley 1581 de 2012 and Decreto 1377 de 2013

Compiled in Decreto Único Reglamentario 1074 de 2015 (DUR), Libro 2, Parte 2, Título 2, Capítulo 25 (formerly Decreto 1377).

- **Health data is sensitive data.** Art. 5 defines sensitive data as data that affects privacy or whose misuse can cause discrimination. It names health, sex life and biometric data. A cosmetology record is sensitive data. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/ley_1581_2012.htm)
- **Processing sensitive data is prohibited unless an exception applies (art. 6).** The exceptions are explicit authorization, vital interest of an incapacitated person, certain nonprofit bodies acting for their members, judicial claims, and anonymized historical, statistical or scientific use. For a salon, only explicit authorization applies in practice. The Constitutional Court (C-748 de 2011) calls this consent "previo, expreso e informado." (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/ley_1581_2012.htm)
- **Extra duties for sensitive data (Decreto 1377 art. 6 = DUR 2.2.2.25.2.3).** The controller must tell the client that answering questions about sensitive data is optional. No service may depend on the client giving sensitive data. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/decreto_1377_2013.htm)
- **Authorization must be recorded in a form that can be consulted later (art. 9).** The client may ask for proof of the authorization (art. 8). The product must keep consent records. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/ley_1581_2012.htm)
- **Data subject rights (art. 8).** Know, update and correct data; request proof of authorization; be informed of the use; complain to the SIC; revoke authorization or request deletion; free access. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/ley_1581_2012.htm)
- **Deletion (DUR 2.2.2.25.2.6).** Deletion does not apply when a legal or contractual duty requires keeping the data. The controller must offer free, easy channels for requests. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/decreto_1377_2013.htm)
- **Retention (DUR 2.2.2.25.2.8).** Keep data only as long as is "razonable y necesario" for the stated purpose, unless a legal or contractual duty requires more. No fixed number of years applies to salons. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/decreto_1377_2013.htm)
- **Privacy policy minimum content (DUR 2.2.2.25.3.1).** (1) controller identity, address, email and phone; (2) processing and purpose; (3) data subject rights; (4) the person or area that handles requests; (5) the procedure to access, update, correct, delete and revoke; (6) effective date and database validity period. (https://normas.cra.gov.co/gestor/docs/decreto_1377_2013.htm)
- **RNBD registration (Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos).** Decreto 090 de 2018 limits the duty to companies and nonprofits with total assets above 100,000 UVT, plus public entities. Natural persons and smaller businesses are exempt from registering but must still comply with the law. New registrable databases must be registered within two months of creation. (https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/consultorio/y-en-que-termino-el-registro-nacional-de-bases-de-datos-2601424)
- **RNBD deadlines for registered controllers.** Annual update from January 2 to March 31. Substantial changes within the first 10 business days of the next month. Security incidents within 15 business days of detection. (https://sigi.sic.gov.co/SIGI/files/mod_documentos/documentos/CS005-I01/versiones//CS005-I01_copia_no_controlada.pdf)
- **SIC draft RNBD resolution (2026).** The SIC published a draft on May 27, 2026 that changes RNBD update terms. Adoption: UNVERIFIED. (https://www.cerlatam.com/normatividad/sic-proyecto-de-resolucion-27-may-2026/)
- **SIC position on health data.** The SIC treats health data as sensitive and requires authorization. It has issued administrative orders on health data. No SIC concept specific to estética was found. (UNVERIFIED) (https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/comunicado/mediante-orden-administrativa-la-sic-busca-blindar-el-tratamiento-de-la-informacion-relacionada-con-la-salud-y-las-decisiones)
- **Software provider role (inference).** The product likely acts as an "encargado" (processor) for the salon, which is the "responsable." The contract between them (contrato de transmisión) and hosting location (international transfer rules) need legal review. Section 1.7.

### 1.2 Rules for esthetic and cosmetology establishments

- **Ley 711 de 2001 (scope of the cosmetologist).** Art. 8 allows facial cleansing, facial and body massage, hair removal, manual lymphatic drainage, and other procedures that need no medication prescription, surgery, "procedimientos invasivos," or acts reserved to health professionals. Art. 7 prohibits acts reserved to physicians. Art. 6(e) requires a medical certificate when the client shows visible disease or the cosmetologist has doubts. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/ley_0711_2001.htm)
- **Resolución 2263 de 2004 (opening and operating requirements).**
  - Art. 1 parágrafo: establishments doing therapeutic techniques, surgery, tattoos, piercing or implants must follow the rules for health service providers.
  - Art. 2 defines "procedimiento invasivo" as a procedure performed by a physician that aggresses the body chemically or mechanically, or uses intradermal or subcutaneous injections.
  - Art. 5.1(r): every cosmetic product must have a registro sanitario or a notificación sanitaria obligatoria (NSO).
  - Art. 5.5: the establishment keeps process manuals and an equipment maintenance log. It does not require a per-client record.
  - Art. 6: the establishment informs clients in advance about care, precautions, risks and costs of each service. This is the closest rule to informed consent. (https://www.minsalud.gov.co/sites/rid/Lists/BibliotecaDigital/RIDE/DE/DIJ/Resoluci%C3%B3n_2263_de_2004.pdf)
- **Resolución 3924 de 2005.** Makes the inspection guide for these centers mandatory. Its Appendix 1 table marks which technologies belong to the cosmetologist's competence. The table is an image; it marks microdermoabrasión, dermabrasión, láser, IPL and carboxiterapia as "NO" for cosmetologists, and diatermia as "SI." It does not name peels by depth, RF, HIFU, microneedling, hydrafacial or cryolipolysis. The guide asks about cosmetic registration of products, not client records or consent. (https://normograma.invima.gov.co/normograma/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minproteccion_3924_2005.htm) and the Bogotá health secretariat reading of the annex (https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/Documents/Normativa_sanitaria_centros_esteticos.pdf)
- **MinSalud Concepto 1929032 (2024, non-binding).**
  - It states that cosmetology establishments are not obligated to keep a historia clínica.
  - It calls laser hair removal a medical procedure that uses high-risk devices.
  - It confirms that departmental and municipal health secretariats supervise these centers, not the Ministry. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/concepto_minsaludps_1929032_2024.htm)
- **Habilitación.** A center that provides health services must register with the departmental secretariat and go through habilitación. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/concepto_minsaludps_1192881_2022.htm)
- **Ley 1799 de 2016.** Prohibits medical and surgical esthetic procedures for people under 18, even with parental consent. Superficial chemical and mechanical peels and laser hair removal are excepted. The Constitutional Court declared art. 3 conditionally constitutional (C-246 de 2017); the condition was not verified. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/ley_1799_2016.htm)
- **Consent for minors.** Consent must be written for minors under the Bogotá guidance. (https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/sitios/SectorBelleza/Galera%20de%20descargas/Publicaciones/LINEAMIENTOS%20SDS%20EVENTOS%20BELLEZA%202011%20-%2013-07-2011.pdf)
- **Bogotá Resolución 723 de 2010 (annex for estética).** Lists a valoración record, treatment plan, consent and evolution notes. Whether it still applies: UNVERIFIED. (https://www.saludcapital.gov.co/SectorBelleza/Galeria%20de%20Descargas/Acreditación/Resolución_723/Res_723_de_2010_Anexo_2_Estandares_Estética.pdf)
- **Peel strength, IPL and injectables.** No rule sets a limit by peel strength or depth. No MinSalud concept on IPL was found. Injectables fall under "invasive" and are for physicians only. Peel limits and IPL status: UNVERIFIED beyond the 3924 table and Concepto 1929032.
- **Cosmetic products.** Decreto 219 de 1998 sets the national cosmetics regime. CAN Decisión 833 defines the NSO as a sworn notice to INVIMA before a cosmetic goes on sale. (https://sice.oas.org/trade/junac/decisiones/dec833_s.pdf)

### 1.3 Health record scope: is a salon a "prestador de servicios de salud"?

- **Resolución 1995 de 1999** applies to "todos los prestadores de servicios de salud." Resolución 839 de 2017 sets a minimum 15-year retention from the last visit. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/resolucion_minsaludps_0839_2017.htm)
- **Salons are not covered.** MinSalud Concepto 1929032 says cosmetology establishments do not have to keep a historia clínica. Centers that do medical procedures must go through habilitación and then follow Res. 1995. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/concepto_minsaludps_1929032_2024.htm)
- **Ley 2015 de 2020 (historia clínica electrónica).** Obligates health service providers. Requires the electronic record to carry the treating professional's firma digital. Res. 866 de 2021 lists providers, EPS and insurers as covered actors. Salons fall outside both. (https://apps.procuraduria.gov.co/gd_1952/docs/l2015020.htm)
- **Draft rule (2026).** A MinSalud draft in public consultation from July 24, 2026 would repeal Res. 1995 and Res. 839 and add "proveedores y operadores de sistemas de información en salud" to its scope. Adoption: UNVERIFIED. (https://www.minsalud.gov.co/Normativa/anexosproyectos/ANEXO2MEMJUSTIFICATIVAHISTORIA%20CLINICA%2022%20JUL%2026_20260722151201.pdf)
- **Implication.** The product's record must meet the Habeas Data and consent rules. It does not have to meet the Res. 1995 historia clínica rules, unless a tenant clinic with habilitación uses it or the draft rule passes. Design the record so it can meet Res. 1995 later (see Section 9).

### 1.4 Electronic signatures and consent

- **Ley 527 de 1999.**
  - Art. 5: data messages cannot lose legal effect only because they are electronic.
  - Art. 7: a signature requirement is met if a method identifies the signer, shows approval of the content, and is reliable and appropriate for the purpose.
  - Arts. 11–12: courts weigh how reliably the message was generated, stored and kept intact.
  - Art. 28: a digital signature is presumed to show the signer's intent. (https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/ley_0527_1999.htm)
- **Decreto 2364 de 2012 (DUR 2.2.2.47.x).**
  - Art. 1 lists electronic signature methods such as codes, passwords, biometric data and cryptographic keys.
  - Art. 4: a signature is reliable if (1) the signature-creation data belongs only to the signer, and (2) any later change to the message can be detected. Reliability may also be proven by other means.
  - Art. 5: an electronic signature has the same validity as a handwritten one.
  - Art. 7: mechanisms the parties agree on are presumed valid unless proven otherwise. (https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/decreto_2364_2012.htm)
- **MinSalud Concepto 201511601694961 (2015).** Consent can be electronic. Decreto 2364 is technology-neutral. Res. 1995 art. 18 accepts codes or other means that replace a signature, provided the record cannot be changed after saving. (https://www.minsalud.gov.co/Normatividad_Nuevo/Concepto%20Jur%C3%ADdico%20201511601694961%20de%202016.pdf)
- **Código General del Proceso art. 244** presumes data messages are authentic. (https://procesal.uexternado.edu.co/codigo-general/articulo-244-documento-autentico/)
- **Ley 2213 de 2022** covers court filings and powers of attorney sent by data message. It does not govern clinical consent. (https://www.ramajudicial.gov.co/documents/36615802/142533470/10TrasladoRecurso202300066.pdf/1d82ace4-1f56-a306-c41b-00326e23de42)

**Answer (from the research, pending lawyer review).** A drawn signature on a touch screen plus audit data is likely valid as a simple firma electrónica. The drawn image alone is weak on Decreto 2364 art. 4(1), because the creation data must belong only to the signer. Strengthen the record with:

- identity verification (ID number plus a one-time code sent to the client's phone or email);
- a hash of the final document and a trusted timestamp;
- storage that prevents later changes;
- a copy sent to the client;
- a clause in the terms stating that the parties accept electronic signature (Decreto 2364 art. 7).

**Signature tiers.**

| Tier | Legal basis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn signature + audit trail + OTP + hash | Ley 527, Decreto 2364 | Likely valid as firma electrónica simple. Lower evidentiary weight. |
| Firma digital (certificate from an ONAC-accredited entity) | Decreto 333 de 2014 | Gets the art. 28 presumption. Stronger, not required. (https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/decreto_0333_2014.htm) |
| "Firma electrónica certificada" (vendor term) | Legal definition: UNVERIFIED | Ask certification vendors for the legal basis before using the term. |

### 1.5 Facturación electrónica (DIAN)

- The salon issues invoices, not the record software. Res. 165 de 2023 art. 7 obligates sellers of goods and services. Art. 8 excludes certain natural persons who are not IVA-responsible. (https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/resolucion_dian_0165_2023.htm)
- **Out of scope for v1 (inference).** A record and scheduling product does not issue invoices.
- **If invoicing is added later.** Either the product becomes an authorized proveedor tecnológico, or the salon uses its own habilitated software. Both paths need a digital certificate, and the salon stays responsible for its invoices. (https://normograma.dian.gov.co/dian/compilacion/docs/oficio_dian_13246_2025.htm)
- Competitors offer DIAN invoicing as a paid add-on or as a core feature of Colombian salon tools (Section 2).

### 1.6 Consumer protection and messaging

- **Ley 1480 de 2011 (Estatuto del Consumidor), art. 47 (retracto).** Applies to distance or non-traditional sales. For services, the withdrawal window is 5 business days from the contract. It excludes services that began with the consumer's agreement. (https://normativa.colpensiones.gov.co/colpensiones/compilacion/docs/ley_1480_2011.htm)
- **Ley 1480, art. 43, num. 5.** A clause that lets the provider keep money when it does not perform the contract is ineffective. (Same source.)
  - (inference) A salon that cancels must refund a deposit. A "no refund" rule fits client no-shows only.
  - (inference) A deposit taken over WhatsApp may count as a distance sale.
- **Ley 2300 de 2023.** Mainly about debt collection (art. 1). Art. 5 extends its rules to advertising messages, including messaging apps. (https://normativa.colpensiones.gov.co/colpensiones/compilacion/docs/ley_2300_2023.htm)
  - Art. 3 sets contact hours: Monday to Friday 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.; Saturday 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.; no Sundays or holidays.
  - Art. 5 par. 3 applies those hours to advertising messages.
  - Art. 5 par. 2 bars forcing a buyer to accept commercial messages and requires an easy opt-out.
  - (inference) Appointment reminders are probably not advertising, so the hours rule may not cover them. Keep marketing messages inside the hours and collect explicit opt-in.

### 1.7 Open questions for a Colombian lawyer

1. If a tenant clinic with habilitación uses the product, does it become subject to Res. 1995, Ley 2015 and the draft rule's information-system provider duties?
2. What retention period should salon records have when no historia clínica duty applies? Should it follow a civil-liability limitation period?
3. Does a drawn signature plus OTP plus document hash meet Decreto 2364 art. 4 for consent to procedures with equipment?
4. Laser hair removal is listed as "aparatología" in Res. 2263, but MinSalud calls it a medical procedure. Which reading do local secretariats apply?
5. Is the product a processor (encargado) under Ley 1581? What must the transmission contract say? Does hosting data outside Colombia trigger international transfer rules?
6. Is there any rule on cosmetologists applying superficial peels, by acid type or concentration?
7. Does the RNBD apply to the salon, given its asset level? Does it apply to the product as a provider?

- AgendaPro is the only broad competitor with COP plans, consent on Premium, and DIAN as an add-on. Its Premium plan (COP 150,000/mo) is the entry tier for consent and ficha.
- Gaps across all opened tools: audit log, data export, signed consent with photo-use consent. Pabau and Reservo prices need a sales quote. Several signature claims need direct verification.

### 2.5 Standard features for a SaaS of this kind

Standard in general SaaS practice (inference; not sourced):
- Account and profile (user, business profile, locations)
- Settings (services catalog, schedules, business hours, rooms or equipment)
- Notifications (email, WhatsApp or SMS reminders)
- Billing and subscription management (plans, trials, invoices from the vendor)
- Data export (client data, records, consents)
- Team management (roles, permissions, professional schedules)
- Audit log (who viewed or changed a client record and when). Required for a health-data product. No opened competitor shows one clearly.

## 3. Common esthetic treatments in Colombia

Legend:
- **(s)** = suggested. Inference or industry convention, not sourced.
- Prices are COP per session from published listings (Fresha, AgendaPro, Doctoralia, Bogotá and Medellín unless stated). They are not 2025 surveys. Listings contain typos; outliers were dropped.
- **Scope** = who may perform it in Colombia, based on Ley 711 de 2001, Res. 2263 de 2004 and the Res. 3924 de 2005 table.

### 3.1 Limpieza facial profunda
- Duration: 60–90 min. Course: about 1 per month (s).
- Price: COP 45,000–150,000 in Bogotá; COP 20,000–35,000 at basic salons; COP 180,000–250,000 with devices. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/facials/in/co-bogotá-cundinamarca; https://agendapro.com/mp/co/facial-bogota)
- Contraindications (s): active herpes or infection, recent isotretinoin, sunburn, severe inflammatory acne.
- Record (s): skin type; products and steps; extraction areas; steamer or high-frequency settings; reactions.
- Scope: cosmetologist. (Ley 711 art. 8, https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/ley_0711_2001.htm)

### 3.2 Peeling químico (superficial, medium)
- Duration: 20–40 min. Course: superficial peels 4–6 sessions every 2–4 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 150,000–280,000 for the face (Cali directory; no Bogotá data). (https://agendapro.com/mp/co/peeling-cali)
- Contraindications: pregnancy and lactation (absolute). Isotretinoin: 6 months (Mayo Clinic) or 12–24 months (Medigraphic), conflicting. Active herpes (Mayo). Keloids (s). (https://www.medigraphic.com/files/derma/cd-2025/1/cd251b-T1.html; https://www.mayoclinic.org/es/tests-procedures/chemical-peel/about/pac-20393473)
- Record: agent; concentration; vehicle; volume; exposure time; application mode (https://docs.bvsalud.org/biblioref/2016/10/2286/2013_58.pdf). Also pH, number of layers, neutralization, frost level (s).
- Scope: **gray** for superficial. Medium peels: physician-only (s). A chemical peel is "chemical aggression" under Res. 2263 art. 2, but no norm names peels by depth.

### 3.3 Microdermoabrasión
- Duration: 30–45 min. Course: 4–6 sessions (s).
- Price: no reliable Bogotá price. One 2024 Medellín blog reports about COP 650,000. (https://medium.com/@wannajuana/microdermabrasion-peels-co2-laser-and-more-botox-in-medellin-2024-c33f1ca28612)
- Contraindications: active infection (herpes, HPV); caution with keloids; rosacea relative. Isotretinoin about 6 months (secondary sources). (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535383/)
- Record (s): tip or crystal type; vacuum level; passes; skin response.
- Scope: Res. 3924 table marks microdermoabrasión and dermabrasión "NO" for cosmetologists. Salons offer it anyway. Treat as **gray to physician-only**.

### 3.4 Dermaplaning
- Duration: 30 min. Course: every 3–4 weeks (s).
- Price: no COP data found.
- Contraindications (s): active acne; rosacea; sunburn; oral anticoagulants; keloid history. (weak source: https://www.scribd.com/document/464789279/dermaplaning)
- Record (s): blade type and lot; angle; areas; post-care product.
- Scope: **gray**. Not named in any norm. Mechanical blade procedure.

### 3.5 Hidrafacial / hydrafacial
- Duration: 30–60 min. Course: monthly (s).
- Price: COP 150,000–380,000. One clinic lists COP 365,000. (https://agendapro.com/blog/tratamientos-faciales-mas-pedidos/; https://agendapro.com/site/co/doctorallanosclinicadepiel/208959)
- Contraindications (s): sunburn; active infection; rosacea flare; recent isotretinoin. (vendor source: https://belislaser.com/faqs/for-whom-is-a-hydrafacial-treatment-not-suitable)
- Record (s): tip; serum brands and lot; vacuum level; extraction.
- Scope: **gray**. Vacuum therapy is "NO" in the Res. 3924 table. Hydrafacial not named.

### 3.6 Radiofrecuencia facial / corporal
- Duration: 30–45 min. Course: 6–10 sessions, weekly (s).
- Price: COP 80,000–350,000 (regional AgendaPro average, not Colombia-specific). Body combos COP 115,000–138,000. (https://agendapro.com/blog/radiofrecuencia-facial-tipos-y-funcionamiento/)
- Contraindications: pacemaker and implanted electronic devices; pregnancy and lactation; metal implants in the area; active cancer; uncontrolled epilepsy. (same AgendaPro blog; https://www.sanitas.es/biblioteca-de-salud/estetica/tratamientos-y-aparatologia/radiofrecuencia)
- Record (s): device and probe; frequency; energy or temperature target; passes; duration; area.
- Scope: **gray**. Res. 3924 lists diatermia (microwave and high frequency) as "SI." RF not named.

### 3.7 Microneedling / dermapen
- Duration: 30–60 min. Course: 3–6 sessions every 4 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 120,000–450,000 in Bogotá; COP 580,000 in Cali. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/microneedling/in/co-bogotá; https://agendapro.com/mp/co/dermapen-cali)
- Contraindications: active acne; herpes or infection; eczema or psoriasis (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459344/). Keloids; bleeding disorders or anticoagulants; uncontrolled diabetes; oral isotretinoin (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7764156/).
- Record: needle depth per zone and needle length. A vendor chart gives 0.1–0.25 mm for periorbital and perioral zones and 1.0–1.5 mm for scars (https://pabau.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/microneedling-depth-chart.pdf). Also cartridge lot; speed; serum; bleeding endpoint (s).
- Scope: **physician-only** (s). Skin puncture fits the Res. 2263 "invasive" definition. No norm names microneedling.

### 3.8 Plasma rico en plaquetas (PRP)
- Duration: 60–90 min. Course: 3 sessions every 4 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 250,000–650,000. (https://www.doctoralia.co/tratamientos-servicios/plasma-rico-en-plaquetas-prp/medellin)
- Contraindications: infectious, oncologic and hematologic comorbidities (2025 consensus, musculoskeletal context: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12104799/). Thrombocytopenia, anticoagulants, pregnancy (s).
- Record (s): blood volume; kit and lot; centrifuge settings; activation; volume applied; adverse events.
- Scope: **physician-only**. Intradermal injection is "invasive" under Res. 2263.

### 3.9 Depilación con cera
- Duration: 15–45 min. Course: every 3–5 weeks (s).
- Price: axilas COP 10,000–20,000; media pierna COP 25,000–48,000; pierna completa about COP 55,000; cuerpo completo about COP 100,000. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/underarm-waxing/in/co-bogot%C3%A1; https://agendapro.com/mp/co/henna-bogota)
- Contraindications: isotretinoin (6–12 months per secondary sources); topical retinoids; sunburn; varicose veins in the area; diabetes (clearance); herpes or open wounds (weak study guide: https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nc-esthetician/hair-removal-makeup/waxing-safety-contraindications).
- Record (s): wax type and brand; temperature test; area; skin reaction.
- Scope: cosmetologist. Ley 711 art. 8 names hair removal. (https://normograma.supersalud.gov.co/compilacion/docs/ley_0711_2001.htm)

### 3.10 Depilación láser / IPL
- Duration: 15–60 min. Course: 6–8 sessions every 4–8 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 49,900 (small zone) to COP 250,000 (unlimited zones) in Bogotá (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/laser-hair-removal/in/co-bogotá). Medellín leg prices (COP 24,000–57,000) look suspect.
- Contraindications: pregnancy (sources conflict); isotretinoin, 1 to 6 months (sources conflict); photosensitizing drugs; tanning in the last 4–8 weeks. (https://www.skininc.com/treatment/medspa/article/21882029/top-5-tips-for-safe-effective-laser-hair-reduction)
- Record (s): Fitzpatrick type; wavelength; fluence; pulse width; spot size; cooling; test spot. Darker skin calls for Nd:YAG and lower fluence (https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507861).
- Scope: **physician-only** per Res. 3924 table (láser and IPL "NO") and MinSalud Concepto 1929032 ("procedimiento médico").

### 3.11 Lifting de pestañas
- Duration: 45–60 min. Course: every 6–8 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 85,000–120,000 in Bogotá. (https://agendapro.com/mp/co/lifting-de-pestanas-bogota)
- Contraindications: conjunctivitis; styes; blepharitis; recent eye surgery (https://londonlash.com/blogs/news/understanding-contra-indications-contra-actions-when-to-say-no-to-lash-extensions). Patch test advised (https://health.clevelandclinic.org/are-lash-lifts-safe).
- Record (s): lift and glue brand and lot; shield size; processing time per step; patch test result; reactions.
- Scope: cosmetologist (s). Non-invasive; no norm restricts it.

### 3.12 Laminado de cejas
- Duration: 45–60 min. Course: every 6–8 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 50,000–75,000 in Bogotá. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/brow-lamination/in/co-bogotá-bogotá)
- Contraindications (s): same as lash lift, plus broken skin or dermatitis in the brow area.
- Record (s): product brand and lot; step times; tint; patch test; skin reaction.
- Scope: cosmetologist (s).

### 3.13 Diseño de cejas / henna
- Duration: 30–45 min. Course: every 2–4 weeks (s).
- Price: COP 12,000–34,000 in Bogotá. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/henna-brows/in/co-bogotá)
- Contraindications (s): henna or dye allergy; skin infection; recent isotretinoin (if waxing).
- Record (s): henna brand and lot; shade; mapping; patch test; hair removal method (wax, thread, tweezers).
- Scope: cosmetologist (s). Includes depilación under Ley 711.

### 3.14 Masaje reductor
- Duration: 45–60 min. Course: 10–12 sessions (s).
- Price: no direct listing found. Related body combos COP 115,000–138,000.
- Contraindications (s): pregnancy; thrombosis; acute infection; heart or kidney failure; open wounds.
- Record (s): zone; technique and pressure; oil or gel; circumference measurements.
- Scope: cosmetologist. "Masajes faciales y corporales" is in Ley 711 art. 8.

### 3.15 Drenaje linfático manual
- Duration: 45–60 min. Course: 8–10 sessions (s).
- Price: COP 80,000–150,000 typical; range COP 45,000–290,000. (https://www.doctoralia.co/tratamientos-servicios/drenaje-linfatico-manual/bogota)
- Contraindications: active infection; deep vein thrombosis; heart failure; severe kidney disease. Cancer is listed by one source. (https://www.ocu.org/salud/bienestar-prevencion/informe/drenaje-linfatico)
- Record (s): zones; sequence; pressure; edema or measurements; symptoms.
- Scope: cosmetologist. Named explicitly in Ley 711 art. 8.

### 3.16 Maderoterapia
- Duration: 45–60 min. Course: 10–12 sessions.
- Price: one Medellín center lists from COP 80,000 for 30 min (listing URL not confirmed). No Bogotá data.
- Contraindications: kidney or heart conditions; dermatitis; varicose veins; menstruation; sensitive skin (https://www.lanacion.com.ar/salud/vida_sana/el-tratamiento-de-moda-que-moldea-el-cuerpo-y-en-mujeres-acentua-la-cintura-de-avispa-nid02012025/). Pregnancy: no source found.
- Record (s): tools used; zones; pressure; bruising.
- Scope: **gray to cosmetologist**. Manual massage. Res. 3924 marks a mechanical massager "SI."

### 3.17 Carboxiterapia
- Duration: 15–30 min (https://www.doctoralia.co/tratamientos-servicios/carboxiterapia). Course: 8–10 sessions (s).
- Price: no COP data found.
- Contraindications: pregnancy and lactation; severe cardiovascular disease; anticoagulants; infection; epilepsy (clinic sources: https://www.tuasaude.com/es/carboxiterapia/; no clinical guideline found).
- Record (s): CO2 flow rate; volume; needle gauge; sites; adverse events.
- Scope: **physician-only**. "Dióxido de carbono (carboxiterapia subdérmica)" is "NO" in the Res. 3924 table.

### 3.18 Mesoterapia
- Duration: 30–60 min (https://www.doctoralia.co/tratamientos-servicios/mesoterapia). Course: 4–10 sessions (s).
- Price: COP 100,000–180,000 body or face; hair or per-vial treatments COP 1.0M–1.8M. (https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/mesotherapy-treatments/in/co-bogotá)
- Contraindications (s): pregnancy; anticoagulants; infection; allergy to components.
- Record (s): product, lot and expiry; dose; needle; depth; sites.
- Scope: **physician-only**. Injections are invasive under Res. 2263; mesotherapy guns are "NO" in the Res. 3924 table.

### 3.19 HIFU
- Duration: 30–90 min. Course: 1–2 sessions per year (listing: https://agendapro.com/mp/co/hifu-bogota).
- Price: COP 149,990 (promo) to 1,500,000; typically COP 280,000–750,000 per zone (same listing).
- Contraindications: pacemaker or metal implants; dermal fillers; pregnancy (https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/9/4958/html). Active infection; severe acne; isotretinoin within 6 months; surgery within 3 months (https://pabau.com/templates/ultrasound-facial/).
- Record (s): cartridge depth; energy; number of lines; zones; device and cartridge lot. No sourced parameter values were found.
- Scope: **gray**. Not in the 2005 table. INVIMA issued an alert on a counterfeit Liposonix ultrasound in beauty centers. (https://app.invima.gov.co/alertas/ckfinder/userfiles/files/ALERTAS%20SANITARIAS/Dispositivos_Medicos/2019/Mayo/Alerta%20No_%20%23092-2019%20-%20Sistema%20de%20Ultrasonido%20LIPOSONIX%20Falsificado%20.pdf)

### 3.20 Criolipólisis
- Duration: about 60 min per zone. Course: 1–3 sessions per zone every 4–6 weeks (vendor listing; treat as (s)).
- Price: COP 980,000 for 4 zones; COP 1,100,000 for a 100-minute session. (Listings; URLs and cities not confirmed.)
- Contraindications: Raynaud's; cryoglobulinemia; cold agglutinin disease; paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria; cold intolerance (https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/21060-cryolipolysis). Pregnancy and lactation (vendor source only). Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia is a rare risk to disclose; Cleveland Clinic notes it is more common in men and in people of Hispanic ethnicity.
- Record (s): applicator size; temperature; duration; vacuum; zone; photos and measurements.
- Scope: **gray**. Not in the 2005 table. Equipment needs INVIMA registration.

### 3.21 Common data fields across treatments

**Client-level (s, with the Bogotá consent guidance as basis for the consent part):**
- ID; age; Fitzpatrick type
- medications (isotretinoin date; anticoagulants; photosensitizers)
- allergies; pregnancy or lactation; diabetes
- implants or pacemaker; herpes history; keloid history
- prior treatments
- antecedentes and diagnóstico; consentimiento informado with cuidados and precauciones (Res. 3924 / Bogotá guidance)

**Product-level:** brand; active ingredient; concentration; lot; expiry. Cosmetics need an NSO. Equipment needs a registro sanitario. (https://sice.oas.org/trade/junac/decisiones/dec833_s.pdf)

**Equipment:** model; registro sanitario; maintenance log (hoja de vida del equipo). (Res. 2263 art. 5.5)

**Session:** date; professional; zone; duration; parameters; skin response; adverse events; photos; post-care instructions; next appointment (s).

**Pre-treatment:** patch test or test spot for dyes, lifts, lasers (s).

**Gaps:** No COP prices found for dermaplaning or carboxiterapia. No Bogotá price for maderoterapia or masaje reductor. No sourced parameter ranges for HIFU, RF or laser. Several contraindication sources are commercial or vendor pages.

## 5. Typical workflow

### 5.1 Independent cosmetologist

No Colombian source gives the end-to-end flow. Steps below combine cited sources with inference.

1. **First contact.** WhatsApp or Instagram message. (inference)
2. **Booking and deposit.** Payment link, Nequi, or Bre-B key. (Section 4)
3. **Reminder.** 24–48 hours and 2–3 hours before. (https://agendapro.com/blog/confirmaciones-automaticas-por-whatsapp/)
4. **Valoración inicial.** Skin type; phototype; active lesions; hydration; home products (for example, retinol). (https://www.noticiasrcn.com/salud-y-bienestar/como-saber-cuando-un-procedimiento-estetico-en-el-rostro-es-seguro-1073697)
5. **History intake.** Allergies; medications; pregnancy; previous procedures; "before" photos. (https://agendapro.com/blog/historia-clinica-estetica/)
6. **Informed consent,** signed before the procedure. (https://agendapro.com/blog/historia-clinica-estetica/) Res. 2263 art. 6 requires advance information about care, precautions, risks and costs.
7. **Treatment plan.** Sessions, schedule, products, cost. The number of sessions follows the valoración. (https://agendapro.com/blog/historia-clinica-estetica/; https://agendapro.com/site/2/centrodeessamiesvalledupar/312614)
8. **Session record.** Date; treatment; product or equipment; zone; observations; immediate reaction. (https://agendapro.com/blog/historia-clinica-estetica/)
9. **Product use logged per session.** Can feed stock and retail. (inference)
10. **Balance payment,** after deducting the deposit. (inference; Mexican example in Section 4.2)
11. **Post-care instructions.** Hydration; sun protection; expected effects (for example, micro-scabs that fall off in 3–10 days). (https://agendapro.com/mp/cl/centros-medicos-providencia) (Chilean clinic)
12. **Control and follow-up.** Follow-up photos; discharge; maintenance plan. (https://agendapro.com/blog/historia-clinica-estetica/)
13. **Rebooking and feedback messages** after the visit. (https://agendapro.com/blog/confirmaciones-automaticas-por-whatsapp/)
14. **Reviews and referrals.** (inference; no source found)
15. **Cancellations and no-shows.** Policy per Section 4.2.

### 5.2 Multi-staff salon

Reception and assignment steps are inference unless noted.

1. **First contact** reaches reception or a shared WhatsApp number. (inference)
2. **Assignment.** Reception assigns a cosmetologist by service, skill and availability. (inference) AgendaPro configures per professional. (https://agendapro.com/blog/por-que-usar-un-sistema-de-comisiones-automatizado-en-tu-salon-ventajas-y-como-implementarlo/)
3. **Steps 2–9 of the independent flow apply.** The client file is shared. Each session is attributed to the cosmetologist who performed it. (inference)
4. **Checkout by reception.** (https://agendapro.com/blog/consejos-para-recepcionistas-de-salones-de-belleza/)
5. **Commissions per professional.** Models: percentage per service; percentage on product sales; goal-based; tiered; mixed. (https://agendapro.com/blog/por-que-usar-un-sistema-de-comisiones-automatizado-en-tu-salon-ventajas-y-como-implementarlo/)
6. **Staff status.** Employees, independent contractors, or chair renters. AgendaPro warns that chair rental creates labor risk when the person is in fact subordinate. (https://agendapro.com/blog/abrir-un-salon-de-belleza-en-colombia/)
7. **Reminders, post-care, rebooking, cancellation policy** follow the independent flow from one shared system. (inference) A client may move to another cosmetologist when the original is absent. (inference)

## Implications for product design

- **Consent is the core feature.** Sensitive data needs explicit, prior, recorded consent (Ley 1581 art. 6, 9). Build consent as a versioned document with a signature event, audit data, and proof the client can request (art. 8(b)).
- **Signature design.** Plan for a drawn signature plus identity verification (OTP to phone or email), a document hash, a trusted timestamp, and immutable storage. Offer a certified firma digital as an upgrade path. Do not promise legal validity; state that the salon's lawyer must confirm the level required for each procedure.
- **Make answers to sensitive questions optional** and show the right message on the form (DUR 2.2.2.25.2.3). Do not block booking when a client declines to answer.
- **Protect records as health data.** Use encryption at rest and in transit, role-based access, and an audit log of who viewed or changed each record. No competitor opened shows a clear audit log, which is a differentiator.
- **Build the data-subject rights into the product.** Export, correction, revocation and deletion requests need in-app paths. Deletion must respect any legal duty to keep the data.
- **Retention is a setting, not a constant.** Set default retention per tenant with a legal review. Do not hard-code 15 years, which applies to historia clínica under Res. 839 and does not apply to salons (Concepto 1929032).
- **Privacy policy and RNBD.** Ship a policy with the six minimum elements (Section 1.1). Ask the salon whether it must register in the RNBD. The product should help the salon answer the "responsable" questions.
- **Design the record for later compliance.** A tenant clinic may need Res. 1995 and Ley 2015 later. Keep structured fields for diagnosis, professional identity, and signature, so the record can meet those rules if the draft rule passes.
- **Treatment-specific forms, not one generic ficha.** Each treatment needs its own structured fields (Section 3). Examples: peel (agent, concentration, pH, layers, exposure time, neutralization); laser (Fitzpatrick, wavelength, fluence, test spot); lash lift (product lot, patch test). Let the salon configure fields per service.
- **Flag scope limits in the UI.** Some treatments are physician-only or gray-zone (Section 3). Show a warning when a cosmetologist records a physician-only procedure, and let the salon record the medical responsibility.
- **Contraindication checks at intake.** Pregnancy, isotretinoin date, anticoagulants, pacemaker and similar fields feed warnings before a session.
- **Minors.** Block or flag appointments for people under 18 that involve medical or surgical esthetic procedures (Ley 1799). Require written consent for minors.
- **Payments and deposits.** Support Bre-B and Nequi keys as manual reference and payment-link flows first. Do not build a payment rail in v1. Do not hard-code a deposit percentage; let the salon set it and show the refund rule (Ley 1480 art. 43).
- **Messaging.** Send appointment reminders by WhatsApp or SMS by default. Limit marketing messages to opt-in contacts and to Ley 2300 hours (Section 1.6). Charge for messaging only if the cost model supports it. Competitors charge for WhatsApp: AgendaPro from COP 10,000/mo for 50 messages; Fresha COP 50–230 per message. Users complain about these costs.
- **Multi-staff from day one.** Each session needs a performing professional. Record commissions data only if the salon wants it; defer commission payouts.
- **Keep v1 out of invoicing.** Note the DIAN integration as a future option (Section 1.5). Competitors sell it as an add-on, so a later partner integration is a reasonable path.
- **Data export is table stakes.** Export client records, consent files and session data in open formats. Most competitors do not state this clearly, so state it in your own product.
- **Pricing benchmark.** COP 30,000–150,000/mo covers the Colombian competitors' entry plans (AgendaPro, ImpsoSalon, Jeloda). Verify Fresha's COP figures before using them.
- **Do not overbuild.** Keep the first release to: agenda and reminders; client profile; configurable ficha; treatment cases with per-session records; consent with signature and audit; deposits and payment links; basic reports; data export. Add inventory, loyalty and marketing later if users ask.
